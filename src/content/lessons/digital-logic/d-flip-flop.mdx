Every register, counter and CPU pipeline is built from this one part. Once you know *when* it looks at its input, you can read any timing diagram you'll meet.

## How it behaves

A D flip-flop copies its input `d` to its output `q`, but only at the **rising edge** of the clock. Between edges, `q` holds whatever it had. That's memory: one bit of it.

<Note term="Non-blocking assignment">The <code>&lt;=</code> in <code>q &lt;= d;</code>. It means "on this edge, q becomes d". Use it for anything clocked.</Note>

## Reading the waveform

Find each rising edge of `clk`. Look straight down to `d`. Whatever `d` is at that instant is what `q` becomes, until the next edge. Anything `d` does *between* edges is invisible to the flip-flop.
