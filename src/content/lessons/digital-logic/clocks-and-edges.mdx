Gates answer instantly, which is a problem: a long chain of them settles at different times, and halfway through the answer is garbage. Hardware fixes this with a **clock**.

## The heartbeat

A clock is a wire that switches between 0 and 1 at a steady rate, forever. Every part of a design agrees to only *look* at its inputs at one moment of each tick. In between, signals are allowed to wobble and settle.

<Note term="Rising edge">The instant the clock goes from 0 to 1. In Verilog it's written <code>posedge clk</code>. Most hardware acts on rising edges.</Note>

## Period and frequency

The **period** *T* is the time from one rising edge to the next. The **frequency** is how many periods fit in a second: *f* = 1 / *T*.

A 100 MHz clock ticks 100 million times a second, so its period is 10 ns. A 3 GHz CPU core has a period of about 0.33 ns. In that time light travels roughly 10 cm.

## Why edges matter

Because everything samples on the same edge, millions of parts can agree on what happened during one cycle. The next lesson shows the part that does the sampling: the flip-flop.
